## Design synthesis

The scorecard combines several evidence-informed practices rather than claiming that one named productivity system is universally optimal:

- Use no more than two quarterly focus goals to protect attention and expose opportunity cost.
- Translate desired outcomes into specific, controllable weekly behaviors.
- Pair important behaviors with implementation intentions: when or where cue X occurs, perform action Y.
- Record progress physically or digitally and review it weekly.
- Treat time management as both objective allocation and subjective experience.
- Protect recovery, chosen relationships, and basic health-supporting routines as part of performance capacity.
- Adjust or disengage from a failing strategy instead of treating persistence as inherently virtuous.
- Use points as descriptive feedback, with autonomy and meaning preserved; avoid competition, streak punishment, or endless target escalation.

The exact `60:40` weights, 20% unplanned substitution limit, and sustainability caps are explicit design judgments for this workflow. They are not thresholds established by the cited studies. Test them against user experience at each quarter review.

## Evidence base

Accessed 2026-07-31.

1. **Specific goals and feedback.** Locke and Latham's review reports that specific, challenging goals with feedback generally outperform vague “do your best” goals, subject to commitment, ability, task complexity, and resources. Use this to make weekly commitments observable without assuming difficulty is always beneficial. Source: https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgPsych/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20%26%20Latham%20%282002%29.pdf

2. **Progress monitoring.** A meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies found that interventions increasing monitoring frequency improved goal attainment, with larger effects when progress was recorded or reported. This supports lightweight logs and a weekly close. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/

3. **Implementation intentions and mental contrasting.** Meta-analytic evidence supports connecting a goal to anticipated obstacles and an if-then action plan. This supports calendar cues, coping plans, and minimum versions rather than intention alone. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/

4. **Time management and wellbeing.** A meta-analysis covering 158 studies and 53,957 participants found moderate associations with performance, academic achievement, and wellbeing, with a somewhat stronger relationship to wellbeing. A later workplace intervention review found limited and inconsistent experimental evidence, so avoid overclaiming any specific scheduling technique. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7799745/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13112514/

5. **Recovery.** Meta-analyses link psychological detachment, relaxation, mastery, and control during non-work time with better wellbeing and recovery outcomes. This supports a non-negotiable sustainability layer rather than treating rest as leftover time. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5233687/ and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/job.2217

6. **Goal flexibility.** A 2025 meta-analytic review found goal-striving flexibility associated with better wellbeing and functioning and less illbeing. This supports stop, reduce, redesign, and re-engagement conditions. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12932095/

7. **Social connection.** The WHO Commission on Social Connection identifies social connection as important to health and wellbeing. This supports deliberate relationship behavior as a scored foundation without prescribing a particular social style. Source: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978240112360

8. **Health-supporting defaults.** WHO guidance supports regular physical activity and a diet grounded in adequacy, balance, moderation, and diversity. CDC guidance states that adults generally need at least seven hours of sleep, while individual needs and clinical circumstances vary. Use these only as discussion defaults; prefer user-approved and clinician-provided targets. Sources: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet, and https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/data-research/facts-stats/adults-sleep-facts-and-stats.html

9. **Gamification caution.** Reviews of health and wellbeing gamification note that points, badges, and leaderboards alone may fail to support intrinsic motivation. Preserve choice, competence feedback, relatedness, and meaningful reasons for goals. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6617915/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6096297/

10. **Dietary self-monitoring limits.** Reviews suggest food self-monitoring can support behavior change in some contexts, but protocols and adherence vary and self-reported intake is imperfect. Keep logging low burden and score pre-agreed behaviors rather than moralizing foods. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8928602/

## Boundaries

- Do not present this score as a diagnostic, medical, nutritional, or mental-health assessment.
- Do not infer calories, nutrients, sleep disorders, addictions, or relationship quality from sparse logs.
- Do not recommend deprivation, compensate food with exercise, or reinforce compulsive tracking.
- Escalate urgent safety concerns and encourage qualified help when appropriate.
- Revisit the scoring design after one quarter or sooner if it increases anxiety, concealment, rigidity, or overwork.
