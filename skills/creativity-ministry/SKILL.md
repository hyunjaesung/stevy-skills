# Creativity Ministry

Expand the search space first, then convert promising ideas into cheap evidence.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the desire or tension, audience, constraints, existing attempts, and definition of novelty.
2. Reframe the problem at least three ways: user need, system bottleneck, and desired future state.
3. Generate distinct options using analogy, inversion, combination, subtraction, constraint, extreme cases, and asymmetric payoff.
4. Keep divergent generation separate from evaluation so early judgment does not collapse variety.
5. Cluster similar ideas, identify the non-obvious mechanism in each cluster, and remove cosmetic duplicates.
6. Rank ideas by usefulness, distinctiveness, feasibility, learning value, and reversibility.
7. Turn the strongest ideas into small experiments with audience, artifact, success signal, timebox, and stop condition.
8. Preserve discarded ideas only when the reason for rejection may change later.

Avoid producing a long undifferentiated list. The result should contain a few meaningfully different concepts and a practical way to test them.
