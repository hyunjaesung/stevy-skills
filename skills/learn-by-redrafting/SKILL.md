# Learn by Redrafting

Learning is evidenced by what the learner can produce after studying, not by how much material was consumed.

## Core Loop

1. **Define the task.** Capture the exact question, expected format, rubric, deadline, and allowed resources.
2. **Write V0 unaided.** The learner answers from memory before studying. Preserve gaps and uncertainty instead of repairing them for the learner.
3. **Diagnose.** Label missing concepts, weak reasoning, unsupported claims, transfer failures, and communication problems.
4. **Study narrowly.** Select only the material needed to address the diagnosed gaps. Use examples, retrieval, and comparison rather than passive rereading.
5. **Write V1 from scratch.** Hide V0. The learner produces a new answer without copying or line-editing the first draft.
6. **Compare V0 and V1.** Identify new understanding, remaining blind spots, accidental memorization, and regressions.
7. **Grill one question at a time.** Ask the highest-leverage question, wait for the learner's answer, then adapt the next question. Include counterexamples and unfamiliar applications.
8. **Write V2 personally.** The learner authors the final response. Offer critique, structure, or a rubric, but do not silently replace their work.
9. **Schedule retrieval.** Review weak ideas after approximately 1, 7, and 30 days, adjusting intervals based on performance.

## Session Record

Keep a compact record containing:

- Assignment and rubric
- V0, V1, and V2 or links to them
- Diagnosed gaps
- Study sources
- Grill questions and answers
- Mastery evidence
- Next retrieval date

If the user provides a vault or course folder, use it for continuity. Otherwise return a portable Markdown record. Never claim mastery solely because the final prose sounds polished.
